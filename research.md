## Working Papers

<div class="paper paper--jmp">
  <h3 class="paper__title"><a href="{{ '/francois_jmp.pdf' | relative_url }}">Where Should Science Funding Go? Estimating Marginal Returns to Public Funding Across Research Fields</a></h3>
  <p class="paper__meta"><span class="paper__badge">Job Market Paper</span> &middot; Francois Bares &middot; September 2026</p>
  <details class="paper__more">
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    <p>I estimate the marginal returns to public research funding across 19 scientific fields using NIH R01 grants and OpenAlex publications. Knowledge spillovers explain much of the variation in returns. Counterfactual allocations based on disease burden or marginal returns produce more science across three welfare-relevant measures than the current allocation.</p>
  </details>
</div>

<div class="paper">
  <h3 class="paper__title"><a href="{{ '/Xprize_30_09_2026.pdf' | relative_url }}">Innovation Prizes as Fundraising Signals: Evidence from XPRIZE Competitions</a></h3>
  <p class="paper__meta">Francois Bares, Thomas Hellmann, Elaine Hungenberg, and Andrew Tauhert &middot; September 2026</p>
  <details class="paper__more">
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    <p>Using PitchBook data on startups participating in XPRIZE competitions and a synthetic difference-in-differences design, we find that participation raises the quarterly probability of raising equity by 7.7 percentage points. The effect is larger for firms without patents and for larger prizes. Participation also attracts investors new to the relevant technology area; public grant funders respond less than private investors.</p>
  </details>
</div>

<div class="paper">
  <h3 class="paper__title"><a href="{{ '/AlphaFold_nature%20(3).pdf' | relative_url }}">Does Artificial Intelligence Democratize Science? Evidence from AlphaFold</a></h3>
  <p class="paper__meta">Francois Bares, Anantha Divakaruni, and Ludovic Phalippou &middot; September 2026</p>
  <details class="paper__more">
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    <p>We study the diffusion of AlphaFold across universities. Following its public release, lower-ranked institutions gain a larger share of protein research publications in top journals, particularly those without high-performance computing facilities. The evidence is consistent with open-access AI reducing the resources needed to conduct frontier protein research.</p>
  </details>
</div>
