## Job Market Paper

### Where Should Science Funding Go? Estimating Marginal Returns to Public Funding Across Research Fields

[Paper (PDF)](/assets/papers/where-should-science-funding-go.pdf)

This paper estimates how public research funding should be allocated across 19 biomedical fields. A structural model of scientists competing for grants and producing knowledge with spillovers reveals large differences in marginal returns across fields. Allocating funds according to these estimated returns would produce 39&ndash;54% more output than the current allocation and 15.1&ndash;26.4% more than an allocation aligned with disease burden.

**Presented at:**

- Oxford FAME seminar
- NBER productivity seminar
- BDI&ndash;EIEF&ndash;CEPR Conference on Finance and Innovation

## Other Papers

### Does Artificial Intelligence Democratize Science? Evidence from AlphaFold

_With Anantha Divakaruni and Ludovic Phalippou._

[Paper (PDF)](/assets/papers/does-ai-democratize-science.pdf)

Using university publication data around AlphaFold's open release, this paper studies whether artificial intelligence broadens access to frontier science. In the two years after release, the share of top-journal protein research produced by the average lower-ranked university rose by 50% relative to the average top-ranked university. Lower-ranked universities also produced more novel work, and those with in-house structural biologists relied less on collaboration with top-ranked universities.

### Innovation Prizes as Fundraising Signals: Evidence from XPRIZE Competitions

_With Thomas Hellmann, Elaine Hungenberg, and Andrew Tauhert. Preliminary draft._

[Paper (PDF)](/assets/papers/innovation-prizes-as-fundraising-signals.pdf)

Using PitchBook financing data for 383 startups and a synthetic difference-in-differences design, this paper estimates that XPRIZE participation increases the quarterly probability of raising equity by 7.7 percentage points, from a 4.7% pre-launch baseline. The effect is larger for firms without patents, and participants attract investors new to the relevant technology area; the analysis finds no evidence that prizes crowd out adjacent technologies.
